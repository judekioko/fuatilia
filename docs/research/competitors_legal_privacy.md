## 2. Data protection: Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 (DPA) and ODPC rules

Primary sources: DPA text (ODPC copy) https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/TheDataProtectionAct__No24of2019.pdf ; Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021 https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf ; Registration Regulations 2021 https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2021/265/eng@2022-12-31 ; ODPC Cross-border Guidance Notes 2026 https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ODPC-%E2%80%93-Guidance-Notes-for-Cross-border-Data-Transfers.pdf

### 2.1 What the data is
- **Sensitive personal data** (DPA s.2) covers data revealing *health status, biometric data, **property details**, marital status, family details* (names of children, parents, spouses), among others. [V] (DPA s.2; repeated in the ODPC 2026 cross-border guidance p.23)
  - Medical and insurance claim documents count as **health data**, and so does an NHIF/SHA claim [V].
  - Payslips and bank or M-Pesa statements very likely reveal "property details", and often family details such as next of kin or dependants. **Treat them as sensitive** [U as an interpretation; the safest reading].
  - ID documents contain ordinary personal data. A photo or fingerprint used for **identification** is biometric data, which is sensitive [U].
- The likely result is that **almost all of Fuatilia's core data is sensitive personal data.**

### 2.2 Registration with the ODPC
- No one may act as a data controller or processor unless registered (s.18(1)). Thresholds are set by the Data Commissioner based on industry, volume, and **whether sensitive data is processed** (s.18(2)). [V]
- Registration Regulations 2021: **exempt** only if annual turnover is below KSh 5m **and** there are fewer than 10 employees, **unless** the activity is in the mandatory-registration schedule. The schedule includes health administration/patient care, financial services, property management, telecoms, direct marketing, transport and more. [S] https://mmsadvocates.co.ke/registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-in-kenya-minimum-thresholds/ ; https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-data-protection-getting-ready-for-registration/
  - Fuatilia is not obviously in a listed sector [U]. Even so, given the volume of sensitive data and the reputational stakes, **register anyway from day one.**
- **Fees** [S, same sources]: micro/small (up to 50 staff, up to KSh 5m turnover) KSh 4,000, renewal KSh 2,000; medium KSh 16,000 / 9,000; large KSh 40,000 / 25,000. The certificate is valid **24 months**. Register at the ODPC portal (https://www.odpc.go.ke).
- The application must describe the data, purposes, categories of data subjects, risks and safeguards, and **measures to indemnify data subjects** (s.19(2)). [V] Knowingly giving false details is an offence (s.19(3)). [V]

### 2.3 Lawful basis and consent
- Principles (s.25): lawful and transparent processing, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, and **no transfer outside Kenya without proof of adequate safeguards or the data subject's consent** (s.25(h)). [V]
- **Sensitive data** may only be processed where s.25 is met **and** a s.45 ground applies. The relevant one is s.45(c)(i): processing is *"necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of a legal claim"*. [V] This fits disputes and complaints well, but it does not obviously cover a stuck application with no claim attached [U]. For that case, rely on explicit consent as well.
- **Health data (s.46)** may only be processed by or under a health care provider, **or by a person under a duty of professional secrecy or confidentiality "under any law"**. [V] **This is the hardest provision for Fuatilia** [U]. Mitigations need counsel's view:
  - the user keeps their own data, encrypted client-side, so Fuatilia does not "process" its contents;
  - an advocate partner owes the professional secrecy duty;
  - or medical claims are excluded until legal advice is obtained.
- Consent (s.32 and s.2 definition): the controller **bears the burden of proving consent**. Consent can be **withdrawn at any time**. It is not freely given if the service is conditional on consent to processing not needed for the service. [V] The ODPC defines consent as "express, unequivocal, free, specific and informed". [V] (cross-border guidance p.4)
- Duty to notify (s.29): before collecting data, tell users their rights, the purpose, recipients, security safeguards, and any transfer out of Kenya. [V]
- Children (s.33): parental or guardian consent is required. Add an age gate (18+) or a guardian flow. [V]

### 2.4 Data protection impact assessment (DPIA)
- Required before high-risk processing (s.31(1)). It must describe the processing, its necessity and proportionality, the risks, and the mitigations (s.31(2)). [V]
- General Regulations reg.49 lists high-risk processing. Fuatilia triggers several items: **(e) processing sensitive personal data or data relating to vulnerable groups; (f) combining datasets from different sources; (i) innovative use of new technology (AI drafting); possibly (g) large scale.** [V] **A DPIA is mandatory.** The template is in the Third Schedule (reg.50). [V]
- If the DPIA shows **high residual risk**, consult the Data Commissioner **before** processing (s.31(3)). [V] DPIA reports are to be **"submitted sixty days prior to the processing"** (s.31(5)). [V] **Plan a 60-day regulatory lead time before launch.** Firms differ on whether every DPIA must be filed [S] https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2022/Practice/TMT/tmt-alert-31-january-Unpacking-the-Data-Protection-General-Regulations-2021-of-the-Data-Protection-Act-24-of-2019 ; assume it must be.

### 2.5 Security and breach notification
- Data protection by design and by default (s.41): pseudonymisation, encryption, access control. [V]
- **Breach (s.43):** if data is accessed by an unauthorised person and there is a real risk of harm, notify the **Data Commissioner within 72 hours** of becoming aware. A late notice must give reasons. Tell **affected users in writing within a reasonably practical period.** [V]
  - A processor (e.g. the cloud host) must tell the controller **within 48 hours** where practicable (s.43(3)). [V]
  - Telling users is **not required if the data was encrypted** with appropriate safeguards (s.43(6)), a strong reason for encryption at rest. [V]
  - Keep a breach register recording the facts, effects and remedial action (s.43(8)). [V]

### 2.6 Cross-border storage (EU/US hosting)
- s.48: a transfer abroad requires proof to the Data Commissioner of appropriate safeguards, or a necessity ground (contract with the data subject, a legal claim, etc.). [V]
- **s.49(1): sensitive personal data may be processed outside Kenya only with the data subject's consent AND confirmation of appropriate safeguards.** [V] The ODPC 2026 guidance says this needs **explicit consent**, written into the contract and transfer documentation, with stricter safeguards for sensitive data. [V]
- Reg.40 bases: appropriate safeguards (legal instrument), adequacy decision, necessity, or consent. **The ODPC has published Standard Clauses** for controller-to-controller and controller-to-processor transfers (Annex 15.4/15.5 of the 2026 guidance). [V] **The ODPC 2026 guidance also says controllers transferring data out of Kenya are required to notify the ODPC of the transfer.** [V] No published adequacy list was found [U].
- **Data localisation (reg.26 / s.50):** applies only to "strategic interest" processing such as civil registration, elections, public finance, protected systems, basic education, and primary/secondary health care. [V] Fuatilia is not covered, so hosting in the EU or US is **legally possible**, with explicit transfer consent and safeguards (ODPC standard clauses with AWS, GCP or Azure).
- Practical option: host in-region (a Kenyan data centre, or AWS Cape Town [U]) to avoid the transfer regime for sensitive data. Either way, do a transfer impact assessment and record onward transfers. [V]
- **AI drafting:** if user case text or documents go to a foreign LLM API, **that is a cross-border transfer of (often sensitive) data.** Treat the AI provider as a processor: standard clauses, explicit consent, and data minimisation such as redacting ID or account numbers before the call [U, design recommendation].

### 2.7 Data subject rights and timelines
- s.26: rights to be informed, access, object, correct, and delete false or misleading data. s.35: right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects. s.38: portability. s.40: rectification/erasure. [V]
- General Regulations timelines [V]:
  - **access within 7 days**, free of charge, in electronic form if requested electronically (reg.9);
  - **rectification within 14 days**, and refusal with reasons within 7 days (reg.10);
  - **portability within 30 days**, at a fee capped at cost (reg.11);
  - **erasure response within 14 days** (reg.12).
- Retention (s.39): keep data only as long as necessary. Set case-closure deletion defaults. [V]

### 2.8 Enforcement exposure
- Administrative fine up to **KSh 5m or 1% of prior-year turnover, whichever is lower** (s.63). [V]
- Compensation to data subjects (s.65). [V] By Jan 2026 the ODPC had issued 184 compensation orders and 20 penalty notices from 9,061 complaints. [S] https://www.dawan.africa/news/odpc-issues-184-compensation-orders-to-data-protection-complainants-a-milestone-for-privacy-rights-in-kenya . Whitepath and Regus Kenya were fined KSh 5m each. [S] https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/kenya-govt-data-protection-regulator-fines-mobile-loans-lender-whitepath-for-breaching-data-protection-laws/
- Appeals go to the High Court (s.64). [V]

## 3. Legal limits: Advocates Act (Cap. 16)

Text checked against the LSK copy (Rev. 2012): https://lsk.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/AdvocatesAct_Cap16-1.pdf . Kenya Law has a 2024-04-26 consolidation (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/1989/18/eng@2024-04-26), which returned 403 when fetched. An **Advocates (Amendment) Bill 2025** was published 19 June 2025 (https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/bill/na/2025-06-19/the-advocates-amendment-bill-2025/eng@2025-06-19). **Whether either changes s.34 was not verified [U].** Check with counsel.

### 3.1 The rules
- **s.9:** only a person who is admitted, on the Roll, **and** holds a current practising certificate is qualified to act as an advocate. [V] "Unqualified person" means anyone not qualified under s.9 (s.2). [V] A company cannot be an advocate.
- **s.31:** an unqualified person shall not *act as an advocate*, or cause process to issue, or "institute, carry on or defend any suit or other proceedings **in the name of any other person**" in any court. The penalties are contempt, being unable to recover costs, and an offence. [V]
- **s.33:** it is an offence to pretend to be an advocate, or to use any "name, title, addition or description" implying legal qualification. [V] **Branding like "AI lawyer", "wakili wa mfukoni" or "robot advocate" is risky.**
- **s.34(1):** no unqualified person shall, "directly or indirectly, take instructions or draw or prepare any document or instrument" that is:
  - (a) conveyancing;
  - (b) company formation;
  - (c) partnership;
  - (d) probate/administration;
  - (e) a document for which a fee is prescribed under the Remuneration Order (s.44);
  - (f) "**relating to any other legal proceedings**".

  Nor shall they "accept or receive, directly or indirectly, **any fee, gain or reward**" for it. [V] Exempt: public officers on duty, **persons employed by an advocate acting within that employment**, and persons merely engrossing (typing up). [V] Fees received can be recovered as a civil debt (s.34(2)). It is an offence (s.34(3)), with a general penalty of up to **KSh 100,000 and/or 2 years** (s.85). [V]
- **s.35:** s.34 documents must be endorsed with the drafter's name and address. Registries refuse unendorsed ones. [V]
- Case law: *National Bank of Kenya v Anaj Warehousing* [2015] KESC 4. The Supreme Court held that s.34 does not void a document drawn by an advocate without a practising certificate. The commentary reading is that a document drawn by a **non-advocate** is invalid. [S] https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kesc/2015/4 ; https://www.oraro.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Legal-Kenyan-Issue-2.pdf

### 3.2 Analysis for Fuatilia [U: interpretation, needs an advocate's opinion]
- **Complaint letters to a company, landlord, employer or government agency (pre-dispute correspondence)** fall outside the listed categories (a) to (d). Whether they "relate to legal proceedings" (f) depends on context:
  - an ordinary complaint, follow-up letter, Ombudsman complaint or regulator complaint is **probably not** a document "relating to legal proceedings";
  - a **formal demand letter threatening suit**, a Small Claims statement of claim, an affidavit or a court pleading **probably is**.
- **Self-help by the user is lawful.** Anyone may write their own letters and act in person (Small Claims s.20). The risk comes when *Fuatilia* "takes instructions" and "draws or prepares" a document **for** the user, especially **for a fee**. A fixed, generic template that the user fills in is the strongest position (publishing legal information). Bespoke AI drafting from the user's facts looks more like "taking instructions and preparing", so the risk increases with how much the AI shapes the content.
- **Charging:** s.34 expressly bars receiving "any fee, gain or reward" for prohibited documents. So:
  - **never charge for, or bundle payment with, court/claim documents** (Small Claims forms, affidavits, pleadings, statutory demand letters) unless an advocate prepares them;
  - charging for case organisation (storage, timeline, reminders, evidence vault, Ombudsman/regulator complaint packs) is **lower risk**.
- **"Document assembly" safe harbour:** **no Kenyan statutory safe harbour exists** [U: none found]. The framing helps only if it is true in practice: the user chooses the template, supplies and controls every fact, the tool does not advise on legal merits or strategy, and documents carry "prepared by the user" wording. Do not rely on labels alone. DoNotPay shows regulators look at the substance and the marketing.
- **Partnering with advocates:** the s.34 proviso exempts "any person employed by an advocate". A referral or "advocate-review" tier, where a **licensed advocate (or firm) prepares or reviews and takes responsibility** for litigation documents and endorses them under s.35, is the clean route for paid legal documents.
  - Watch the Advocates (Practice) Rules on fee-sharing, touting and advertising, and the s.36 ban on undercutting the Remuneration Order. These constrain revenue-share and "cheap lawyer" pricing [U: rules text not reviewed].
  - A **flat referral fee from advocates** may breach LSK rules [U]. A subscription fee charged to the user for platform features, with advocates billing the user separately, is safer.
- **National Legal Aid Service (NLAS) and the Legal Aid Act 2016:** "legal aid" includes legal advice and representation, and per the Kituo summary also assistance, dispute resolution, *drafting relevant documents* and awareness [S/U]. Only accredited providers may provide legal aid under the Act. Accredited paralegals work under advocate or accredited-provider supervision and **may not charge** people who qualify for legal aid. [S] https://nlas.go.ke/sites/default/files/2024-03/LEGAL%20AID%20%20ACT%20of%202016.pdf ; https://kituochasheria.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Legal-Aid-Act-2016-version-2020.pdf ; https://grassrootsjusticenetwork.org/resources/kenya-legal-aid-bill-2012/
  - **Do not call Fuatilia "legal aid"** unless accredited.
  - An NLAS or accredited-provider (Kituo) **referral partnership** is viable: a free "send my case file to legal aid" export for users who qualify.
- **AI accuracy duty:** courts are sanctioning fabricated AI citations (see Part 1). Any AI-generated letter must avoid citing case law, or cite only verified statutes from a curated, advocate-checked list [U, design recommendation].
