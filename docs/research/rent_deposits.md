## 1. What the law says

### 1.1 Kenya has no general statute on residential deposits
- No Kenyan statute sets a cap on residential deposits, a refund deadline, interest on deposits, or a duty to give an itemised account. The deposit is governed by the **tenancy agreement and general contract law**. The cases treat it as money the landlord holds and must return, less proven lawful deductions.
  - The High Court classifies a deposit claim as a claim on "a contract relating to money held and received" (Small Claims Court Act s.12(1)(b)). Source: *Muhanda v LP Holdings Ltd* (Civil Appeal E256 of 2023) [2025] KEHC 393 (KLR), 23 Jan 2025, Namisi J. https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kehc/2025/393 ; Kenya Law summary: https://x.com/MyKenyaLaw/status/1884482912092910042
  - The BPRT describes a rent deposit as security, "refundable at the termination of the tenancy" once the premises are in the condition in which they were let. Source: *Keoye v Ngechu & 2 others* (Tribunal Case E1122 of 2023) [2024] KEBPRT 794, as given in the search-result summary at https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kebprt/2024/794/eng@2024-05-27 **[full text not read: 403]**

### 1.2 Checking the claim that 2025 case law requires an itemised written account
**This claim is not verified. It looks like a misattribution.**
- *Muhanda* (2025) decided **jurisdiction only**: deposit refunds belong in the Small Claims Court and unpaid-rent claims do not. The appeal was allowed with costs of KSh 40,000. https://hklaw.co.ke/new-court-ruling-expands-small-claims-court-powers-on-rent-disputes/ ; https://nation.africa/kenya/news/judge-clears-way-for-suits-against-landlords-withholding-deposit-4902290
- In the facts of *Muhanda*, the landlord answered a refund request for KSh 230,000 with a repair bill of KSh 271,857. The tenant said no joint inspection had been done. None of the reports says the court laid down an itemisation rule. (Nation, above.)
- The "itemised written account" wording traces to a property-manager guide, real.co.ke. It cites no statute or case for the rule. https://www.real.co.ke/rent-deposit-disputes-kenya/
- **Use in the app:** describe itemisation as **best practice and an evidential point**. A landlord who cannot prove a deduction will struggle in court, and the Small Claims Court Rules require itemised estimates or receipts for *property-damage* claims, r.5(2). Do not present it as a legal requirement. https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ken199062.pdf

### 1.3 Rent Restriction Act (Cap 296) and the Rent Restriction Tribunal (RRT)
- The Act applies only to dwelling-houses with a **standard rent of KSh 2,500 a month or less** (s.2(1)(c), as amended by Act 5 of 1981). https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ken62996.pdf (Rev. 2010)
- The tribunal's powers in s.5(1) include ordering a refund of sums paid "on account of rent" that are irrecoverable under the Act, with a **2-year limit** (s.5(1)(l)). It also covers recovery of arrears and service charges, and fixing water, light and other service charges (s.5(1)(d)). There is **no express power over deposits**. (Same source.)
- Section 17 bans any "premium or other similar sum … in addition to the rent" as a condition of a tenancy and makes it recoverable. An argument is open that a deposit falls under this ban for a controlled dwelling. **This is my interpretation and not settled law.** (Same source.)
- In practice almost no rents are under KSh 2,500. The RRT loses jurisdiction where rent exceeds the threshold: *R v Chairman RRT ex parte Gatanga Mugitiri Co.* [2021] eKLR, as cited at https://njagaadvocates.com/the-best-forum-for-instituting-a-claim-for-refund-of-security-deposit-from-your-landlord/ ; also https://tta.co.ke/the-jurisdiction-of-the-rent-restriction-tribunal/
- **Use in the app:** route a tenant to the RRT only if the monthly rent is KSh 2,500 or less. Even then, the Small Claims Court is usually simpler.

### 1.4 Landlord and Tenant (Shops, Hotels and Catering Establishments) Act (Cap 301) and the BPRT
- The Act covers **controlled tenancies** of shops, hotels and catering establishments: tenancies not in writing, or written for 5 years or less, or terminable within 5 years other than for breach (s.2). https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ken62538.pdf (Rev. 2012)
- Section 12(4) lets the tribunal "investigate any complaint relating to a controlled tenancy … and make such order thereon as it deems fit". The BPRT relies on this power to deal with deposit disputes. (Same source.) BPRT practice on deposits: *Keoye*, above.
- The BPRT has no jurisdiction over residential lets or non-controlled commercial leases: *Ndege v Mutarura & another* [2024] KEELC 6627, as cited by Njaga Advocates, above.
- **Use in the app:** for a **business tenant on a lease of 5 years or less**, the BPRT is an option and has no monetary ceiling. The Small Claims Court still works for claims up to KSh 1M.

### 1.5 Small Claims Court Act No. 2 of 2016 (Rev. 2021)
Source: https://www.policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/KEN1050.pdf
- s.12(1)(b): jurisdiction over "a contract relating to money held and received". s.12(3): ceiling of **KSh 1,000,000**, set by Act 5 of 2020. The Chief Justice may change it by Gazette notice (s.12(4)). **[UNVERIFIED that no gazette change happened after 2021]**
- s.20(1): a party "shall appear in person". Only if unable to appear can they use a "duly authorised representative", defined in s.2 as next of kin or a close relative, appointed in writing and approved by the Adjudicator. s.20A provides for advocates' fees, so advocates may appear. **No lawyer is needed.**
- s.23: a claim starts with a statement of claim in the prescribed form. s.23(2) allows an oral claim, which a court officer writes down. s.23(7) allows electronic filing.
- s.24: required contents of a claim (see section 2.3 below).
- s.25(2): the respondent must respond within 15 days. The Rules set 14 days, or 30 days if the respondent is outside the court's area.
- s.34: the case must be determined **within 60 days of filing**, judgment must be given within 3 days of the hearing, and no more than 3 adjournments are allowed.
- s.38: appeals go to the High Court **on points of law only**, and that appeal is final. The Act sets no time limit for appealing. In practice the **30 days** under s.79G of the Civil Procedure Act is applied. https://kenyalaw.org/caselaw/cases/view/290870/ **[UNVERIFIED: full text not read]**

### 1.7 Limitation periods
- A contract claim must be brought within **6 years** of the cause of action (Limitation of Actions Act Cap 22, s.4(1)(a)). https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ken128809.pdf ; https://www.sheriaplex.com/kenya-acts/5332-section-4-of-limitation-of-actions-act-cap-22-actions-of-contract-and-tort-and-certain-other-actions . For a deposit, time runs from when the refund fell due: the contractual date, or a reasonable time after the tenant vacated.
- RRT refund applications under s.5(1)(l): **2 years** from payment.
- Small Claims Court: an unserved claim is deemed abandoned **6 months** after filing (r.7(2)). It can be reinstated once, but not if the contract is more than 6 years old (r.7(4)(b)).
- High Court appeal: 30 days (see 1.5).

### 1.8 Utility deposits
- **Kenya Power, postpaid account in the tenant's name:** close the account and settle bills, and the deposit is refunded "within 14 working days" to the **registered account holder** only. https://kplc.co.ke/faq/postpaid-meters ; contact customercare@kplc.co.ke (https://www.kplc.co.ke/faqs). If the account is in the landlord's name, the tenant's claim lies against the landlord, as part of the deposit claim.
- Water-company deposits (e.g. Nairobi City Water): **[NOT RESEARCHED / UNVERIFIED]**.
- A deposit the tenant paid to the landlord for utilities is money held and received, so it can be added to the Small Claims claim. A landlord may lawfully deduct proven unpaid utility bills (secondary sources: https://pangoni.io/blog/guides/security-deposit-kenya).
- RRT tenancies (rent of KSh 2,500 or less): the RRT can fix service charges (s.5(1)(d)).

## 2. Practical steps

### 2.1 Before and at move-out
1. Give notice as the lease requires (often 30 days) and keep proof.
2. Pay final rent and utilities. Get **final meter readings** and receipts.
3. Ask for a **joint exit inspection**. Photograph or video every room with timestamps and get the inspection sheet signed. Return the keys against a written acknowledgement.
4. Ask in writing for the refund to be paid to a named M-Pesa or bank account.
(Guidance from practitioners: https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/tenants-guide-steps-to-take-when-landlords-withhold-your-security-deposit-2025091205071835455 ; https://pangoni.io/blog/guides/security-deposit-kenya)

### 2.2 Demand letter (send once the refund is overdue)
No statute prescribes a deadline. Practice is **7 to 14 days**, with **14 days** most commonly recommended (Pulse, above; https://sheria.org/front-pages/blog/post/rent-deposit-refunds-in-kenya-what-tenants-need-to-know). Suggested contents:
- tenant's and landlord's or agent's names and addresses, the premises, and the tenancy dates
- the deposit amount, date paid and payment reference (M-Pesa code or bank slip)
- the date the tenant vacated, the date the keys were returned, and confirmation that rent and utilities are cleared
- the amount demanded. If the landlord has made deductions, dispute each one and ask for an itemised breakdown with receipts or quotes.
- the payment method and a deadline (14 days)
- a statement that the tenant will otherwise file in the Small Claims Court under s.12(1)(b) for the sum plus costs and interest
- sent by email, WhatsApp, registered post or hand delivery, with proof kept
- a phrase such as "without prejudice save as to costs" is optional
- Do not adopt the "7-day statutory notice under s.93A Land Act" suggested in one firm guide (https://muthiiassociates.com/recovering-your-security-deposit-in-kenya/). It appears to be wrong for this purpose. **[UNVERIFIED: treat as an error]**

### 2.3 Choosing the forum
| Situation | Forum |
|---|---|
| Residential, claim up to KSh 1M (almost all cases) | **Small Claims Court** |
| Residential, rent of KSh 2,500 a month or less | RRT possible, but the Small Claims Court is simpler |
| Business premises, controlled tenancy (lease of 5 years or less, or unwritten) | BPRT (s.12(4) Cap 301) or the Small Claims Court if up to KSh 1M |
| Claim over KSh 1M | Magistrates' Court (a lawyer is advisable). Under r.3(4)-(6), a claimant can abandon the excess to stay in the Small Claims Court; the rule text still says KSh 200,000. |

Under s.13, once a Small Claims claim is filed, the same cause of action cannot be pursued in another court.

### 2.4 Filing in the Small Claims Court
- **Form SCC-1** (statement of claim), signed. File at the court nearest to where the transaction happened or where the respondent lives or works (r.3(2)). Rules: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ken199062.pdf
- The claim must contain the names and addresses of both parties, the nature of the claim, the sum claimed, the orders sought, and enough particulars to show how the sum was calculated (Act s.24).
- **E-filing:** register an Individual account at https://efiling.court.go.ke and activate it by email. Choose Small Claims Court, upload SCC-1 and the evidence as PDF, and pay by M-Pesa. Sources: https://x.com/Kenyajudiciary/status/1825416058351624609 ; https://judiciary.go.ke/e-filing-support/ ; https://thekenyatimes.com/explainers/how-small-claims-court-works-filing-process-cases-covered-and-step-by-step-guide/
- **Fees:** the gazetted Second Schedule (LN 145/2019) sets KSh 200 for claims under 50k, KSh 400 for 50 to 100k, KSh 600 for 100 to 150k and KSh 1,000 for 150 to 200k. It also lists service fees of KSh 100 to 500 and KSh 10 per exhibit. The schedule was written for the old 200k cap. Secondary sources say the current maximum is about **KSh 1,000** for claims up to 1M (https://www.nairobiminibloggers.com/small-claims-court-filing-fees-kenya/). **[UNVERIFIED: current fee bands above 200k. Show the fee as assessed on the e-filing portal.]**
- **Service:** the claimant serves the respondent personally, on an authorised agent, or by post if personal service is impractical. A company is served at its registered office (r.35). The claimant files a **Certificate of Service (Form SCC 5)**. Service must be done within 6 months of filing (r.7).
- **Response:** the landlord has 14 days to respond, or 30 days if outside the court's area (r.9(2)). The landlord may admit the claim, in which case judgment follows (r.10), deny it, or counterclaim, for example for damage.
- **Hearing:** strict rules of evidence do not apply (r.31). Judgment must come within **60 days of filing** (s.34).
- **Enforcement:** if the landlord does not pay, apply for execution, such as attaching movable property (s.39). Execution fees are capped at 10% of the claim (Second Schedule, para 8).

## 3. Evidence checklist
- tenancy agreement, or proof of an oral tenancy such as rent receipts or messages
- **proof the deposit was paid**: receipt, M-Pesa statement or SMS, bank slip
- move-in inventory or condition report and photos
- notice to vacate and proof of delivery
- move-out photos and videos (timestamped), the signed exit inspection, and a key-handover acknowledgement
- rent statement showing no arrears; final utility bills and meter readings; KPLC or water account closure records
- all correspondence: the demand letter with proof of delivery, the landlord's reply, any deduction list or invoices
- for disputed deductions: evidence the item was fair wear and tear or pre-existing, and quotes showing the charges are inflated
- witnesses, such as the caretaker or someone present at handover
- the landlord's correct legal name and address for service. Run a company or business-name search if the landlord is a company (*Muhanda*'s landlord was a limited company).
(Sources: Pulse, above; https://muthiiassociates.com/recovering-your-security-deposit-in-kenya/ ; Small Claims Rules r.5 and r.18 list of documents.)

## 4. Can a non-lawyer service help a tenant prepare their OWN documents?

### Statute: Advocates Act Cap 16, s.34
Text checked in the LSK-hosted Rev. 2012 PDF (https://lsk.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/AdvocatesAct_Cap16-1.pdf) and the Rev. 2017 edition (https://pckamunya.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Advocates-Act.pdf). The 2024 consolidation could not be fetched. **[UNVERIFIED: no later amendment to s.34]**
- s.34(1): "No unqualified person shall, either directly or indirectly, **take instructions or draw or prepare any document** or instrument" in categories (a) to (f). The relevant ones are:
  - (e) a document "for which a fee is prescribed by any order made by the Chief Justice under section 44". The Advocates Remuneration Order prescribes fees for letters of demand, so **demand letters arguably fall under (e).** https://lsk.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/advocates-act-remuneration-amendment-order-2014.pdf
  - (f) a document "relating to any other legal proceedings". This covers a **statement of claim**.
  - The person must also not "accept or receive, directly or indirectly, any fee, gain or reward" for doing so.
- Exceptions: public officers acting in the course of duty (for example the court officer who writes down an oral claim under SCC Act s.23(2)), an advocate's employees, and people "employed merely to engross" (copy out) a document.
- Consequences: the client can recover fees paid as a civil debt (s.34(2)). Breach is an offence (s.34(3)), punishable by a fine of up to KSh 100,000, up to 2 years' imprisonment, or both (general penalty, s.85). Courts strike out pleadings drawn by unqualified persons; see the case list at https://kenyalaw.org/caselaw/cases/view/150422 **[case details not read: 403]**.

### Analysis for the app (no Kenyan ruling on self-help software was found)
- **Lawful:** the tenant drafts and signs their own demand letter and SCC-1. Section 34 targets unqualified persons acting *for others*. A litigant in person is the party, and SCC Act s.20 expects parties to appear in person.
- **Low risk:** publishing generic **information, checklists and blank templates**, including the official SCC-1, which the Judiciary itself publishes (https://judiciary.go.ke/forms/), that the user fills in themselves. This is the closest analogue to Judiciary self-help material. No fee is charged for "drawing" a specific document. **[UNVERIFIED: no case or LSK guidance on DIY template software. This is analysis, not authority.]**
- **High risk:** the service takes the user's facts ("instructions") and **produces a tailored demand letter or claim for a fee**. That fits the words of s.34(1)(e)/(f) closely, and charging for it is the aggravating element.
- **Not allowed:** the service, its staff or an AI agent appearing or acting for the tenant in the Small Claims Court. Only the party, next of kin or a close relative approved by the Adjudicator, or an advocate, may do so (s.20 and s.2).

**Design suggestions:**
1. Make the user the author. The user enters and edits every fact and signs, and the generated text is presented as the user's own draft.
2. Use template assembly and guidance rather than legal advice on the merits, and show a clear "not legal advice" notice.
3. Do not price per document drafted. Use subscription or case-tracking pricing, or make document generation free.
4. Offer referral to an advocate, legal aid or a Huduma or Judiciary help desk for contested deductions or claims over 1M.
5. Get a Kenyan advocate's opinion before launch.
